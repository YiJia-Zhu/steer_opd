Abstract
On-policy distillation (OPD) supplies dense token-level supervision by aligning a student to the teacher’s next-token distribution on its own generated rollouts. Existing works mostly implement top-kk OPD, applying reverse Kullback-Leibler (RKL\mathrm{RKL}) divergence between the teacher’s and the student’s renormalized distributions over teacher top-kk support.
However, the teacher scores student-generated rollouts that are inherently off-policy for itself, leaving the reliability of its next-token guidance unclear. Restricting the divergence to the teacher top-kk support also discards the remaining mass outside that support.
These gaps leave two questions implicit: 1) which tokens to align, and 2) how to align those tokens.
To answer these questions, we propose Overlap-gated On-Policy Distillation (O2PD).
As for 1), we quantitatively analyze the reliability of teacher supervision and find that alignment on the student’s trajectory prefix is more reliable than alignment on the full context. Therefore, our O2PD is applied only to the 20% prefix tokens of each student rollout.
As for 2), we theoretically characterize two problems of top-kk OPD: the Teacher-support Uncovering and the Dominant-Mode Mismatch. Based on these analyses, we propose two support strategies: the Reset Support strategy attaches a reset atom to every token, pooling mass outside the teacher top-kk, and the Support Convergence strategy converges the support to the already aligned intersection. Our O2PD employs the designed overlap-gated α\alpha-divergence to interpolate between mode-covering and mode-seeking for per-token alignment, where the overlap is used as a gate to switch the support and adaptively modulate α\alpha.
Extensive experiments on seven common benchmarks demonstrate the superiority of O2PD, improving average performance by up to 5.29 points compared to OPD.
1 Introduction
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) improves reasoning by assigning advantages from outcome-level correctness Guo et al. (2025); Shao et al. (2024); Hu et al. (2025); Zeng et al. (2025). That signal is sparse along long trajectories: many tokens share one reward, and groups of equally correct or incorrect responses can yield vanishing advantages Yu et al. (2025); Liu et al. (2025); Cui et al. (2025). Knowledge distillation supplies denser targets by matching a student to a teacher’s per-token distribution Hinton et al. (2015), but the usual recipe trains on teacher-generated sequences Kim and Rush (2016). The student is then updated off-policy relative to its own sampler Gu et al. (2024); Agarwal et al. (2024). This introduces a distribution mismatch between training and those generated by the student itself.
On-policy distillation (OPD) reverses that sampling: the student generates the rollout trajectory, and the teacher scores the same rollout, so each token receives a dense, on-policy learning signal Agarwal et al. (2024); Gu et al. (2024); Lu and Thinking Machines Lab (2025); Yang et al. (2026).
The alignment process is conducted by minimizing the divergence between the teacher’s and student’s per-token distributions. For example, reverse Kullback-Leibler (RKL\mathrm{RKL}) is a mode-seeking objective that can effectively capture the teacher’s dominant modes. Existing OPD methods mainly adopt top-kk OPD, minimizing the RKL\mathrm{RKL} on the teacher’s top-kk support atoms for tractable cost.
Figure 1: Framework of our O2PD: (a) Restricting alignment to the prefix, where teacher supervision is reliable. (b) Overlap-gated α\alpha-divergence with Reset Support and Support Convergence strategies.
However, the student-generated rollouts are inherently off-policy for the teacher, so the reliability of teacher supervision remains unclear Fu et al. (2026); Zhou et al. (2026).
Besides, the teacher’s top-kk support atoms are just a subset of the full vocabulary Fu et al. (2026); Anshumann et al. (2025). Restricting the alignment to this subset discards the remaining mass outside that support. Empirically, as the teacher-student overlap decreases, the student more frequently samples tokens outside the teacher’s top-kk support, where the teacher’s supervision is unreachable.
These gaps leave two questions implicit: 1) which tokens to align?, and 2) how to align those tokens?
Along these lines, we first identify a Cross-prefix Asymmetry phenomenon: as student prefixes accumulate, the teacher’s accuracy falls, while as teacher prefixes accumulate, the student’s accuracy rises (§3).
Furthermore, we theoretically characterize two problems of top-kk OPD (§4): 1) Teacher-support Uncovering happens because RKL\mathrm{RKL} on the teacher’s top-kk support fails to pull the student onto teacher-only tokens; thus high-confidence teacher modes stay uncovered when overlap is low;
2) Dominant-Mode Mismatch is that even the two top-kk supports already overlap well, the same objective only matches the renormalized distribution and fails to pin the teacher’s mass on the shared atoms.
To address these issues, we propose an overlap-gated α\alpha-divergence on refined support and reliable prefix for OPD, which we term O2PD (shown in Figure 1 ).
For which, inspired by the Cross-prefix Asymmetry phenomenon, we only optimize the 20% prefix tokens of student-generated rollouts to avoid the off-policy accumulation for the teacher.
For how, we introduce two support strategies: the Reset Support strategy attaches a reset atom to every token, pooling mass outside the teacher top-kk, and the Support Convergence strategy converges the support to the already aligned intersection.
Theoretical analysis shows that Forward KL (FKL\mathrm{FKL}) with Reset Support supplies a first-order covering force on teacher-only atoms, and RKL\mathrm{RKL} with Support Convergence pins the teacher’s mass on the shared atoms rather than leftover-renormalizing them. This motivates our design of overlap-gated α\alpha-divergence, where overlap is used as a gate to switch the support strategies and adaptively modulate α\alpha. For each token, α\alpha is mapped from the teacher-student overlap to control the interpolation between the mode-covering and the mode-seeking.
Our contributions are:
• 
We identify a Cross-prefix Asymmetry: teacher supervision is more reliable on the prefix than on the suffix. We theoretically characterize two shortcomings of top-kk OPD, Teacher-support Uncovering and Dominant-Mode Mismatch (§3, §4).
• 
We propose O2PD, which applies overlap-gated α\alpha-divergence on the reliable prefix tokens. The per-token overlap between teacher and student adaptively modulates α\alpha and switches Reset Support and Support Convergence strategies (§3, §4).
• 
Extensive experiments on seven benchmarks show that O2PD achieves state-of-the-art average performance among compared OPD methods, improving over OPD by up to +5.29+5.29 points across three teacher–student pairs.
2 Related Work
GKD, MiniLLM, and later OPD recipes minimize a token-level divergence loss on student-generated trajectories Gu et al. (2024); Yang et al. (2026); Ko et al. (2025). The standard implementation is top-kk OPD (Agarwal et al., 2024; Lu and Thinking Machines Lab, 2025; Anshumann et al., 2025), which minimizes RKL\mathrm{RKL} over the teacher’s top-kk atoms at every position.
At each token, the teacher and student induce next-token distributions pp and qq on vocabulary VV. Write T=TopK⁡(p)T=\mathrm{TopK}(p), S=TopK⁡(q)S=\mathrm{TopK}(q), I=T∩SI=T\cap S, and overlap ρ=|I|/k\rho=\lvert I\rvert/k ( illustrated in Table 1). Top-kk OPD renormalizes both distributions on TT and minimizes
LT=KL(qT∥pT)=∑a∈TqaTlogqaTpaT,L_{T}=\mathrm{KL}(q^{T}\|p^{T})=\sum_{a\in T}q^{T}_{a}\log\frac{q^{T}_{a}}{p^{T}_{a}},
(1)
where qaT=q⁡(a)/q⁡(T)q^{T}_{a}=q(a)/q(T) and paT=p⁡(a)/p⁡(T)p^{T}_{a}=p(a)/p(T). Mass outside TT does not enter the loss.
A concurrent line of work studies when this recipe succeeds or fails. Because the teacher scores student-sampled rollouts, its next-token guidance can be noisy, unteachable, or mismatched with the student Fu et al. (2026); Zhou et al. (2026); Wang et al. (2026b); Wang et al. (2026a); Yu et al. (2026); Shao et al. (2026). Li et al. show that teacher–student top-kk overlap tracks alignment-level and thinking-pattern consistency, and that high-overlap successful runs can be explained largely by overlap increasing Li et al. (2026).

Other methods intervene on which tokens to optimize and how to lessen the overlap mismatch. For example, TIP selects tokens with high student entropy, and tokens with low student entropy plus high teacher–student divergence, where the student is overconfident and wrong Xu et al. (2026). ESR argues that early tokens do problem framing and strategic planning, and truncates the rollout to a fixed first NN tokens Zhou et al. (2026). EOPD keeps the on-policy trajectory and mixes FKL\mathrm{FKL} into RKL\mathrm{RKL} on high teacher-entropy tokens to avoid diversity collapse Jin et al. (2026).
However, these token filters are heuristic, ESR’s cutoff is a fixed short horizon that does not scale with response length, and EOPD changes the alignment objective without using overlap to decide covering versus pinning. O2PD takes both decisions together: a length-scaled prefix, and overlap-gated α\alpha-divergence with Reset Support and Support Convergence for better alignment.
3 Where Should Distillation Concentrate?
3.1 Cross-prefix Asymmetry phenomenon
Student prefixes are off-policy for the teacher.
To test whether teacher supervision remains reliable on student-generated prefixes, we run a cross-prefix completion protocol on MATH500 with a Qwen3-1.7B student and a Qwen3-4B teacher. When each model generates the entire response, the student and teacher obtain 73.8%73.8\% and 85.8%85.8\% pass@1. We then vary a prefix ratio ℓ\ell: the student continues from a teacher-generated prefix of relative length ℓ\ell, and the teacher continues from a student-generated prefix of the same length. Fig. 2 (a) shows an opposite dependence on ℓ\ell. Student pass@1 increases as the teacher prefix lengthens; teacher pass@1 decreases as the student prefix lengthens, and the decline grows with ℓ\ell.
Aligning prefix raises suffix overlap.
To test whether aligning only those early tokens can raise teacher–student overlap on the rest of the trajectory, we use the same Qwen3-1.7B student and Qwen3-4B teacher and train top-kk OPD on DAPO-MATH-17K with the loss applied only to the first 20%20\% of each rollout. As shown in Fig. 2 (b), we compute mean top-kk overlap between the student and the teacher, grouped by relative-position bin.
Overlap on the untrained suffix bins rises together with the trained prefix, so the prefix-only objective improves alignment for suffix tokens. The 00–20%20\% bin remains the lowest throughout, so the prefix is still the hardest region to match. §5.3 further verifies that aligning on a prefix fraction exceeds full-sequence alignment, and concentrating on the prefix outperforms aligning the suffix.
Misalignment concentrates on the prefix.
As shown in Fig. 2 (c), we plot mean LTL_{T} against relative position at initialization and after full-trajectory top-kk OPD.
Mean RKL\mathrm{RKL} is highest near the start of the response and declines with relative position at both checkpoints. Training lowers the curve along the trajectory, so teacher-student mismatch is alleviated, yet the prefix remains the region of largest mismatch.

3.2 Recipe1: Prefix-only Alignment
Based on this analysis, the prefix is both the most reliable source of teacher supervision and the region of largest remaining mismatch, so these are the tokens most worth aligning. Prefix-only training still raises overlap on the untrained suffix, so withholding suffix tokens from the loss does not leave the suffix unaligned. We therefore replace full-trajectory LTL_{T} by the Where objective ℒwhere(ℓ)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{where}}^{(\ell)}:
ℒwhere(ℓ)=𝔼y∼πs(⋅∣x)∑i=1|y|𝟏[i≤ℓ|y|]LT(i).\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{where}}^{(\ell)}=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_{s}(\cdot\mid x)}\sum_{i=1}^{|y|}\mathbf{1}\bigl[i\leq\ell\,|y|\bigr]L_{T}^{(i)}.
(2)
Suffix tokens are sampled with the student but do not enter the optimization. Because the window is a fraction of |y||y|, the number of aligned tokens scales with response length.
4 How to Align Prefix Tokens
4.1 Teacher-support Uncovering and Dominant-Mode Mismatch
We theoretically characterize two shortcomings of top-kk OPD.
The first failure is Teacher-support Uncovering: minimizing RKL\mathrm{RKL} on TT involves gradient coordinates only on TT, so it therefore assigns no gradient to student-only atoms S∖TS\setminus T, and its first-order force on teacher-only atoms in T∖ST\setminus S is multiplied by student mass.
Lemma 1 (Support restriction).
LT=KL(qT∥pT)L_{T}=\mathrm{KL}(q^{T}\|p^{T}) depends on the student logits only through {za:a∈T}\{z_{a}:a\in T\}. Consequently ∂LT/∂za=0\partial L_{T}/\partial z_{a}=0 for every a∉Ta\notin T. In particular, RKL\mathrm{RKL} on TT has no coordinate on S∖TS\setminus T.
On the simplex over TT, the coordinate gradient of LTL_{T} is proportional to student mass qaTq^{T}_{a}, so the first-order force on T∖ST\setminus S is weak whenever that mass is small (Appendix B.1).
Proposition 1 (Covering channel).
Assume I≠∅I\neq\emptyset, q⁡(I)>0q(I)>0, and q⁡(T∖S)=0q(T\setminus S)=0. Then q⁡(T)=q⁡(I)q(T)=q(I), qTq^{T} vanishes on T∖ST\setminus S, and
∂LT∂ζa=0for all ​a∈T∖S.\frac{\partial L_{T}}{\partial\zeta_{a}}=0\qquad\text{for all }a\in T\setminus S.
In this case LTL_{T} is determined by qIq^{I} only, so the first-order update lives only on already aligned atoms. The covering channel on T∖ST\setminus S is off, and Lemma 1 additionally gives ∂LT/∂za=0\partial L_{T}/\partial z_{a}=0 for every a∈S∖Ta\in S\setminus T. When q⁡(T∖S)q(T\setminus S) is small but nonzero, the covering gradient remains proportional to qaTq^{T}_{a}, so the first-order force on T∖ST\setminus S is weak rather than identically zero.
Proposition 1 is why LTL_{T} does not pull T∖ST\setminus S into SS. Fig. 3 checks this on prefix tokens with initial overlap ρ<0.3\rho<0.3: mean overlap moves only from 0.2650.265 to 0.2820.282 after top-kk OPD training, 87.3%87.3\% of the cohort remain below 0.30.3, and none reach ≥0.6\geq 0.6.

The second failure is Dominant-Mode Mismatch: minimizing RKL\mathrm{RKL} on the full-vocabulary is associated with q=pq=p as a mode-seeking target. Top-kk OPD is not full-vocabulary OPD: its loss LTL_{T} lives on the renormalized simplex of TT.
Proposition 2 (Renormalized match on TT).
On the relative interior {qT:q⁡(T)>0}\{q^{T}:q(T)>0\}, every minimizer of LTL_{T} satisfies qT=pTq^{T}=p^{T}. This matches the teacher only as a distribution on TT. It does not pin q⁡(T)=p⁡(T)q(T)=p(T) and does not constrain V∖TV\setminus T, so it is not q=pq=p on VV.
Remaining mode-mass gaps are therefore a shape mismatch on the support TT, not evidence that the student is insufficiently mode-seeking. Fig. 3 (c) checks tokens that already share the teacher’s top-11: the signed gap q−pq-p on that mode stays positive after top-kk OPD and grows with initial overlap. High overlap does not mean the student has matched the teacher’s dominant-mode mass. Proofs of Lemma 1 and Propositions 1–2 are in Appendix B.
4.2 Recipe2: a reset atom on every token (Reset Support)
Teacher-support Uncovering can be handled by our Reset Support strategy, which attaches a reset atom to every token. The reset atom pools the mass outside TT: teacher reset mass is 1−p⁡(T)1-p(T) and student reset mass is 1−q⁡(T)1-q(T). On the simplex over T∪{r}T\cup\{r\},
πp​(a)=p⁡(a)​(a∈T),πp​(r)=1−p⁡(T),πq​(a)=q⁡(a)​(a∈T),πq​(r)=1−q⁡(T).\pi_{p}(a)=p(a)\ (a\in T),\pi_{p}(r)=1-p(T),\pi_{q}(a)=q(a)\ (a\in T),\pi_{q}(r)=1-q(T).
(3)
FKL\mathrm{FKL} on this simplex is LF=KL(πp∥πq)L_{F}=\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{p}\|\pi_{q}). Without a reset atom, FKL\mathrm{FKL} opens only a covering channel on T∖ST\setminus S. The reset coordinate additionally supplies a pooled suppression channel for S∖T⊂V∖TS\setminus T\subset V\setminus T.
Proposition 3 (FKL covering).
On simplex coordinates πq=softmax⁡(ζ)\pi_{q}=\mathrm{softmax}(\zeta),
∂LF∂ζa=πq​(a)−πp​(a).\frac{\partial L_{F}}{\partial\zeta_{a}}=\pi_{q}(a)-\pi_{p}(a).
If a∈T∖Sa\in T\setminus S, πp​(a)>0\pi_{p}(a)>0 and πq​(a)=0\pi_{q}(a)=0, then ∂LF/∂ζa=−πp​(a)<0\partial L_{F}/\partial\zeta_{a}=-\pi_{p}(a)<0, so a descent step raises ζa\zeta_{a}. Teacher mass on T∖ST\setminus S supplies covering force without requiring the student to already place mass there. The reset coordinate yields ∂LF/∂ζr=qr−pr\partial L_{F}/\partial\zeta_{r}=q_{r}-p_{r} with qr=1−q⁡(T)q_{r}=1-q(T) and pr=1−p⁡(T)p_{r}=1-p(T). When T∖ST\setminus S is large the student typically places substantial mass outside TT, so qr>prq_{r}>p_{r} and a descent step lowers the reset coordinate, a pooled suppression of S∖TS\setminus T. If p⁡(T∖S)>0p(T\setminus S)>0, then LF→+∞L_{F}\to+\infty as qT|T∖S→0q^{T}|_{T\setminus S}\to 0, whereas LTL_{T} remains finite.
The reason to use FKL\mathrm{FKL} when T∖ST\setminus S is large: the covering channel stays open when student mass on T∖ST\setminus S is near zero. RKL\mathrm{RKL} on the same support still multiplies those coordinates by q⁡(a)q(a).
4.3 Recipe3: shrink the support, then RKL\mathrm{RKL} (Support Convergence)
Dominant-Mode Mismatch means that minimizing RKL\mathrm{RKL} on TT matches only qT=pTq^{T}=p^{T}. Attaching a reset atom to TT makes the unconstrained RKL\mathrm{RKL} minimizer πq=πp\pi_{q}=\pi_{p} on T∪{r}T\cup\{r\}. On T∪{r}T\cup\{r\} this is q|T=p|Tq|_{T}=p|_{T}, which pins c=q⁡(T)/p⁡(T)=1c=q(T)/p(T)=1 and closes the scale gap in Proposition 2. Reset Support is therefore already enough for the unconstrained problem.
As overlap rises, T∖ST\setminus S shrinks and the job becomes mode-seeking on the already shared atoms II. The interiors of T∪{r}T\cup\{r\} and I∪{r}I\cup\{r\} coincide only when T=IT=I. Leftover atoms T∖IT\setminus I still carry teacher mass but little student mass. Proposition 1 keeps the RKL\mathrm{RKL} factor on those atoms proportional to q⁡(a)q(a), so leftover student mass ε=q⁡(T∖I)\varepsilon=q(T\setminus I) typically remains below p⁡(T∖I)p(T\setminus I) rather than rising to the unconstrained leftover target. On this slice RKL\mathrm{RKL} on T∪{r}T\cup\{r\} still renormalizes II by leftover teacher mass.
Support Convergence strategy is therefore proposed to restrict the support to II. Leftover teacher mass is folded into the reset atom,
πq​(a)=q⁡(a)​(a∈I),πq​(r)=1−q⁡(I),πp​(a)=p⁡(a)​(a∈I),πp​(r)=1−p⁡(I),\pi_{q}(a)=q(a)\ (a\in I),\pi_{q}(r)=1-q(I),\pi_{p}(a)=p(a)\ (a\in I),\pi_{p}(r)=1-p(I),
and LI,r=KL(πq∥πp)L_{I,r}=\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{q}\|\pi_{p}).
Proposition 4 (Leftover renormalization).
Assume I≠∅I\neq\emptyset, T∖I≠∅T\setminus I\neq\emptyset, q⁡(I)>0q(I)>0, and p⁡(T)<1p(T)<1. Write ε=q⁡(T∖I)∈[0,1)\varepsilon=q(T\setminus I)\in[0,1) and freeze the leftover coordinates. Then RKL\mathrm{RKL} on T∪{r}T\cup\{r\}, as a function of the remaining mass 1−ε1-\varepsilon on I∪{r}I\cup\{r\}, is uniquely minimized at

Hence q⁡(a)>p⁡(a)q(a)>p(a) on II if and only if ε<p⁡(T∖I)\varepsilon<p(T\setminus I). The uncovered face ε=0\varepsilon=0 is the special case q⁡(a)=p⁡(a)/(1−p⁡(T∖I))q(a)=p(a)/(1-p(T\setminus I)). Minimizing RKL\mathrm{RKL} on I∪{r}I\cup\{r\} does not include T∖IT\setminus I; its interior minimizer is q|I=p|Iq|_{I}=p|_{I} and 1−q⁡(I)=1−p⁡(I)1-q(I)=1-p(I). Leftover teacher mass therefore renormalizes the aligned atoms under T∪{r}T\cup\{r\} whenever student leftover mass is smaller than teacher leftover mass, and is folded into the reset atom under I∪{r}I\cup\{r\}.
Proposition 4 is why mode-seeking RKL\mathrm{RKL} uses support I∪{r}I\cup\{r\}: when leftover mass remains below leftover teacher mass, T∪{r}T\cup\{r\} still overshoots pp on II, not only on the exact-zero face. Proofs of Propositions 3 and 4 are in Appendix B.
4.4 Recipe4: Overlap-gated α\alpha-divergence
Low overlap needs the covering force of FKL\mathrm{FKL} on T∪{r}T\cup\{r\} (Proposition 3); high overlap needs RKL\mathrm{RKL} on I∪{r}I\cup\{r\}, where T∪{r}T\cup\{r\} would still leftover-renormalize II (Proposition 4). Overlap ρ\rho selects which of those two jobs a prefix token presents.
We therefore propose overlap-gated α\alpha-divergence: ρ\rho interpolates those two regimes and switches the support strategies.
Let πq\pi_{q} and πp\pi_{p} be the student and teacher distributions on the simplex over the current support AA, either T∪{r}T\cup\{r\} or I∪{r}I\cup\{r\}. For α∈(−1,1)\alpha\in(-1,1), the α\alpha-divergence is
Dα(πq∥πp)=41−α2(1−∑i∈Aπq(i)1−α2πp(i)1+α2).D_{\alpha}(\pi_{q}\|\pi_{p})=\frac{4}{1-\alpha^{2}}\Biggl(1-\sum_{i\in A}\pi_{q}(i)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\pi_{p}(i)^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}\Biggr).
(4)
The endpoints recover the two KL directions used above: Dα→KL(πp∥πq)D_{\alpha}\to\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{p}\|\pi_{q}) as α→1\alpha\to 1, and Dα→KL(πq∥πp)D_{\alpha}\to\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{q}\|\pi_{p}) as α→−1\alpha\to-1. Appendix C verifies the two limits for (4). Intermediate α\alpha interpolates those two vector fields rather than adding a second loss.
On each update, let 𝒫\mathcal{P} be the prefix tokens that enter the loss. Set
low=mini∈𝒫⁡ρi,high=maxi∈𝒫⁡ρi,\mathrm{low}=\min_{i\in\mathcal{P}}\rho_{i},\mathrm{high}=\max_{i\in\mathcal{P}}\rho_{i},

gate=clip⁡((ρ−low)/(high−low),0,1),α=1−2⋅gate.\mathrm{gate}=\mathrm{clip}\bigl((\rho-\mathrm{low})/(\mathrm{high}-\mathrm{low}),0,1\bigr),\qquad\alpha=1-2\cdot\mathrm{gate}.
(5)
The support switches at the median: T∪{r}T\cup\{r\} when ρ<ρ0.5\rho<\rho_{0.5}, and I∪{r}I\cup\{r\} when ρ≥ρ0.5\rho\geq\rho_{0.5}.
We therefore propose the overlap-gated α\alpha-divergence for adaptively aligning each token via ρ\rho:
ℒhow=𝔼y∼πs(⋅∣x)∑i=1|y|Dαi(πq(i)∥πp(i)),\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{how}}=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_{s}(\cdot\mid x)}\sum_{i=1}^{|y|}D_{\alpha_{i}}\bigl(\pi_{q}^{(i)}\|\pi_{p}^{(i)}\bigr),
(6)
where ρi\rho_{i} determines both αi\alpha_{i} and the support of DαiD_{\alpha_{i}} as above.

5 Experiments
5.1 Setup
Models and training dataset.
We use three student–teacher pairs (Table 2). Qwen3-1.7B distilled from Qwen3-4B Yang et al. (2025) is a same-family distillation with a larger teacher. DSR1-1.5B distilled from JustRL-1.5B Guo et al. (2025); He et al. (2025) is a same-size distillation on long trajectories. Qwen3-1.7B distilled from Qwen3-4B-Non-Thinking-RL (NTRL) Yang et al. (2025) keeps the first student and changes only the teacher, isolating teacher strength.
The training set is DAPO-MATH-17K with no additional filtering Yu et al. (2025). The compared baselines are: OPD, ESR Zhou et al. (2026), EOPD Jin et al. (2026), and TIP Xu et al. (2026). OPD is the default top-kk recipe. ESR, EOPD, and TIP are trained with the configurations from their papers.
Evaluation.
For fair comparison, we follow previous evaluation pipeline Fu et al. (2026); Lu and Thinking Machines Lab (2025) and evaluate on seven common benchmarks: AIME24, AIME25, AIME26, HMMT25, Minerva Lewkowycz et al. (2022), Olympiad He et al. (2024), and MATH500 Hendrycks et al. (2021); Lightman et al. (2024). AIME24/25/26 and HMMT25 have few problems and report Avg@8; Minerva, Olympiad, and MATH500 have many more and report pass@1 from a single sample. Reported scores use the best checkpoint of each method.
Implementation.
All methods are trained in VERL Sheng et al. (2025). OPD and O2PD use distillation top-kk size k=16k=16. O2PD applies the loss to a prefix fraction ℓ=0.2\ell=0.2, attaches a reset atom, maps α\alpha from the prefix overlap min and max, and switches named support at the median of prefix overlap: T∪{r}T\cup\{r\} below the median and I∪{r}I\cup\{r\} at or above it.
Further training hyperparameters are in Appendix E, and evaluation details in Appendix F.

5.2 Main distillation
Table 2 reports seven-suite Avg for the three student–teacher pairs. O2PD attains 26.8026.80 with the Qwen3-4B teacher, 39.4839.48 with JustRL-1.5B, and 50.5850.58 with NTRL. On every pair it exceeds ESR, EOPD, and TIP on Avg, and it improves over OPD by +1.89+1.89, +4.81+4.81, and +5.29+5.29.
TIP and ESR raise Avg performance over OPD on all three teacher-student pairs, by up to +3.63+3.63 and +2.36+2.36. This reveals that optimizing a subset of tokens is more effective than optimizing all tokens. However, ESR’s Avg gains remain small, and on some benchmarks ESR falls behind OPD, which demonstrates that dynamically adjusting the number of selected tokens may be necessary on harder problems. EOPD also raises Avg over OPD, by up to +3.18+3.18, by mixing FKL\mathrm{FKL} into RKL\mathrm{RKL} on high-entropy teacher tokens, showing that RKL\mathrm{RKL} alone is insufficient and benefits from an FKL\mathrm{FKL} covering channel.
Instead of applying complex token-selective strategies, we optimize a fixed fraction of each trajectory’s prefix, so the number of aligned tokens scales with the response length. We then use overlap-gated α\alpha-divergence to combine FKL\mathrm{FKL} covering with RKL\mathrm{RKL} mode-seeking and switch the support with overlap. This yields a further Avg gain over ESR, EOPD, and TIP on all three teacher-student pairs.
5.3 Discussion
Method components. We ablate five variants (Fig. 4). w/o How applies the prefix restriction to OPD. w/o Where applies overlap-gated α\alpha-divergence with Reset Support and Support Convergence to the full trajectory. FKL\mathrm{FKL}/RKL\mathrm{RKL} uses FKL\mathrm{FKL} on T∪{r}T\cup\{r\} below the prefix overlap median and RKL\mathrm{RKL} on I∪{r}I\cup\{r\} at or above it. w/o II keeps the prefix and runs overlap-gated α\alpha-divergence on T∪{r}T\cup\{r\} only. w/o rr keeps the prefix and runs overlap-gated α\alpha-divergence on the teacher top-kk TT, without the reset atom.
Figure 4: (a) Method-component ablation studies (%). Each bar removes one component of O2PD. (b) Prefix versus suffix performance. Avg is the mean of Avg@8 on AIME24, AIME25, AIME26, and HMMT25.
On the Qwen3-1.7B student with the NTRL teacher, all five variants lie between OPD and full O2PD on seven-suite Avg (Fig. 4 (a)). w/o How exceeds full-trajectory OPD, so the prefix restriction alone beats applying the loss to every token. w/o Where is higher, so How on the full trajectory contributes more than the prefix restriction alone. Dropping either block leaves a gap to full O2PD.
Figure 5: Mean top-kk overlap trend.
Within How, w/o rr is the largest single-component drop; the reset atom carries the covering channel. With the support already switching with overlap, FKL\mathrm{FKL}/RKL\mathrm{RKL} stays below both w/o II and full O2PD: continuous α\alpha interpolates the two vector fields rather than adding a second loss. Converging the support to I∪{r}I\cup\{r\} as overlap rises is the smallest increment.
Prefix versus suffix. To test whether the teacher provides effective supervision on prefix tokens relative to suffix tokens and to the full rollout, we vary where the loss sits on the full trajectory for both OPD and O2PD.
The prefix sweep applies the loss to the first fraction ℓ\ell of each student response; the suffix sweep applies it to the last fraction. The two methods share the full-trajectory endpoint ℓ=1\ell=1.
Both methods are higher at ℓ=0.2\ell=0.2 than at full-trajectory training (Fig. 4 (b)). Optimizing the suffix pulls down that distillation improvement.
At a matched budget of 0.20.2, the prefix outperforms the suffix for both methods, and suffix-only training falls below the full trajectory. The teacher therefore provides effective supervision on prefix tokens. O2PD remains above OPD at every measured endpoint, and the best point is prefix ℓ=0.2\ell=0.2 with the How recipes.
Overlap dynamics. To test whether O2PD raises teacher–student alignment level, measured by mean top-kk overlap, and whether the prefix fraction ℓ=0.2\ell=0.2 still attains the alignment of full-trajectory distillation, we track that overlap over training on the Qwen3-1.7B student with the NTRL teacher for O2PD at ℓ=0.2\ell=0.2 and ℓ=1\ell=1, together with OPD, ESR, TIP, and EOPD (Fig. 5).
All methods start near 0.610.61, rise in the first few steps, and then plateau through step 200200. Distillation under O2PD at ℓ=1\ell=1 rises faster than at ℓ=0.2\ell=0.2; the prefix run then catches up, and both finish near 0.700.70. That level is above OPD, EOPD, and TIP near 0.680.68, and above ESR. Prefix-only distillation therefore delays alignment and does not lower the level O2PD attains.

6 Conclusion
In this paper, we study two questions in OPD: which tokens to align, and how to align those tokens.
For which, we find that teacher supervision is more reliable on the student prefix than on the suffix. For how, we prove that minimizing RKL\mathrm{RKL} on the teacher top-kk neither covers teacher-only atoms at low overlap nor pins teacher mass on shared atoms at high overlap.
Therefore, we propose O2PD, which concentrates on the alignment of the 20%20\% prefix of each rollout via a designed overlap-gated α\alpha-divergence.
The per-token overlap between teacher and student is used to adaptively modulate α\alpha and switch between Reset Support and Support Convergence.
On seven benchmarks O2PD attains the best average performance among compared methods, improving over OPD by +1.89+1.89, +4.81+4.81, and +5.29+5.29 on the three different student–teacher pairs.

Appendix F Evaluation
Downstream evaluation uses the same decoding and grading pipeline for distilled checkpoints and reference models. The cross-prefix protocol in §3 reports pass@1 on MATH500 and is separate from the settings below.
Every model is decoded with vLLM. Thinking mode is disabled. All problems use the model’s chat template with the user prompt:
{problem} Please reason step by step, and put your final answer within \boxed{}.
Decoding uses temperature 0.60.6, top-p=0.95p=0.95, sampling top-k=20k=20, and a maximum of 31,74431,744 tokens.
A response is graded by extracting the final \boxed{} expression and checking mathematical equivalence against the reference answer with a rule-based verifier. The verifier applies symbolic-equivalence normalization for fractions, radicals, and other equivalent forms. A response without a parseable boxed answer is counted as incorrect.
