Abstract
When a user says “Keep it short”, how short should the LLM’s response be?
More generally, users and applications require different levels of instruction adherence, but prompting does not reliably provide fine-grained control over them.
We therefore study “steerable language models” that provide fine-grained numerical control over the degree to which models follow general instructions specified
in text. However, existing approaches do not jointly offer fine-grained control,
efficiency, and scalability across diverse instructions. To address this gap, we introduce DIAL (Distilled Instruction-Adherence Levels) to achieve all three properties. For fine-grained control, we perform classifier-free guidance (CFG) with a
frozen LLM to provide training signals for on-policy distillation of instruction adherence at different levels. For efficiency, we design a lightweight steering adapter
that uses a classifier over the LLM’s hidden states to compute a closed-form steering edit. The classifier adaptively sets the steering magnitude at each token based
on the target adherence level, while accounting for the geometry of the LLM’s
hidden states to preserve response coherence. For scalability, the classifier is
text-conditioned, allowing a shared adapter to support new instructions without
per-instruction fitting. Training requires only instruction and request text, without labeled responses, external generators, or judges. Evaluation across multiple
LLM families shows that DIAL achieves a competitive balance between concept
expression and response fluency, with concept scores increasing monotonically
across adherence levels. We introduce SteerIF, an instruction-only dataset of
98, 625 concept, persona, verifiable, and descriptive instructions. When trained
on SteerIF, DIAL provides fine-grained control over personas and, when combined with prompting, improves verifiable instruction following. In vLLM, the
LLM steered by DIAL retains 96–99% of the base LLM throughput, with pertoken inference latency approximately matching that of the base LLM.

Introduction
