On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) trains a student on its own reasoning trajectories using token-level supervision from a reference-conditioned teacher initialized from the same model. However, access to the
reference can induce teacher behaviors that undermine independent reasoning in thinking models, leading
to reference hallucinations, where the student cites an unavailable reference to justify reasoning steps, and
reconsideration suppression, where the teacher discourages verification and revision at uncertain steps. We
introduce Steered-Teacher OPSD (ST-OPSD) to jointly address these failure modes through activation steering. ST-OPSD extracts two directions from model-generated reasoning trajectories and applies them to the
teacher’s hidden states during distillation target computation, suppressing reference-invoking behavior and
encouraging reconsideration with marker-family calibration. The student learns from the resulting teacher
distributions without being steered. Experiments on AIME and HMMT show that ST-OPSD improves accuracy
over vanilla OPSD and other interventions while restoring reconsideration and reference-hallucination rates
to base-model levels.

1. Introduction
As illustrated in Figure 1, a student tutors a classmate through a difficult math problem with access to the
reference answer. When the classmate pauses to check a step or hesitates about what to do next, the tutor
dismisses these efforts as unnecessary and tells them to follow the provided solution, pointing to the reference
answer to prescribe the next step without explaining why it works. The classmate may learn to reproduce
the solution without understanding the reasoning behind it or how to apply it to new problems. During an
exam without the reference answer, they may lack an independent reasoning strategy, skip necessary checks,
and ultimately resort to guessing.
Language models face a similar challenge in on-policy self-distillation (OPSD). A student generates
reasoning from the question alone, while a teacher initialized from the same model additionally receives a
reference solution and provides token-level supervision along the student’s trajectory [1]. This trains the
student on contexts encountered during its own generation [2, 3], but reference-conditioned supervision
may also transfer behaviors that undermine independent reasoning.
Specifically, there are two teacher behaviors that contribute to performance degradation in thinking
models under OPSD. First, the teacher invokes privileged information through phrases such as “The example
solution uses...”. The student learns to imitate these citations despite lacking access to the reference,
producing reference hallucinations: reasoning steps justified by an unseen solution rather than independent
derivation. These hallucinations manifest reference-induced shortcuts [4, 5]. Second, knowing the answer
leads the teacher to assign low probabilities to reconsideration markers at uncertain branching points [6,
7]. The resulting reconsideration suppression encourages the student to continue confidently [6, 8],
shortening reasoning without corresponding accuracy gains and degrading performance under longer
reasoning budgets [6].
