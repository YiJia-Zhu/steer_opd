Title: STAR: Counterfactual Dual-World Rollouts and Spatial Target Adaptive Reconstruction for Visual On-Policy Distillation
ArXiv ID: 2611.32472
Date: 2026-11-24
Abstract: Vision-language models often rely on global context and language priors while overlooking answer-determining local details. Privileged visual on-policy self-distillation supervises full-image student rollouts using a frozen teacher that views high-resolution evidence crops. Yet our cross-scale diagnostics show that models retain over half of their full-image accuracy when local evidence is completely masked, indicating that single-image correctness does not guarantee reliance on local evidence, while the need for visual correction also varies across tokens with their spatial attention states. We propose STAR, a visual on-policy self-distillation framework combining independent counterfactual dual-world rollouts with spatial-attention-guided target reconstruction. Local image editing constructs paired worlds with identical questions and backgrounds but distinct answer-determining facts, where a shared student samples trajectories independently to expose contextual shortcuts under image-consistent prefixes. Within each world, the teacher contrasts real and mean-color crops to determine the visual correction direction, while the divergence between student image attention and a teacher-centroid spatial reference adaptively controls token-wise target log-probability reconstruction. Without auxiliary attention losses or test-time tool overhead, STAR improves Qwen3.5 across scales by up to 8.04 percentage points in average accuracy over initialization across six fine-grained benchmarks, reaches OPSD's final accuracy $4.7\times$ faster, and generalizes to out-of-domain reasoning tasks.

Title: The Teacher Is Already Inside: Self-Distilling Looped Language Models Beyond Their Last Loop
ArXiv ID: 2611.32452
Date: 2026-11-24
Abstract: Looped language models apply one shared transformer stack several times per token, and every loop is readable through the shared language-model head. Post-training them is hard. Reward-only reinforcement learning needs a verifier and learns from one sparse signal per response; spreading the reward over loops collapses the entropy of every loop; and cross-loop self-distillation, as in LoopOPD, can only teach intermediate loops, because the last loop cannot learn from its own frozen copy: the loss is zero at initialisation. We observe that a looped model already contains a policy that decodes better than its last loop: the next-token distribution extrapolated from the first loop through the last. ELPSD (Extrapolated-Loop Policy Self-Distillation) turns this distribution into a self-distillation target: a frozen copy of the model computes it, and the model's own last loop is distilled towards it on its own rollouts, using the exact reverse KL at every visited prefix. Teacher and student are the same model, so ELPSD needs no reward, no external teacher and no privileged context, and it leaves rollouts and decoding unchanged. We show that the objective pulls the last loop towards the base model's last loop with weight 1+w and away from the base model's first loop with weight w, and that the teacher must stay frozen, since an idealised teacher recomputed from the current model extrapolates without bound. We evaluate ELPSD on Ouro-1.4B-Thinking across five math benchmarks against the base model, GRPO, RLTT and OPSD. Without any reward, ELPSD raises the average avg@8 from 61.15 to 63.69 and is the best method on all five benchmarks, ahead of GRPO (62.32), which uses a verifier, and the self-distillation method OPSD (61.77), which needs reference solutions.

Title: HuluOPD: Unifying Heterogeneous Medical Specialists into One Lightweight Model with Unlabeled Prompts
ArXiv ID: 2611.32021
Date: 2026-11-24
Abstract: Specialist medical vision-language models (VLMs) are strong within their specialties but hard to unify: their training data are often private, and independently developed specialists cannot be assumed to share an architecture. Title: Rethinking Reasoning Effort Training in Large Language Models: On-Policy Distillation or Reinforcement Learning?
ArXiv ID: 2611.32131
Date: 2026-11-24
Abstract: Large language models have shown strong capabilities on complex tasks, yet how to control their reasoning effort remains a significant challenge for practical deployment. This paper provides a systematic investigation of reasoning-effort training, contrasting reinforcement learning (RL) with on-policy distillation (OPD). We find that compressing reasoning and separating effort tiers are governed by different signals. Length-penalized RL reliably compresses a single policy, reducing thinking on AIME25 by 70\% without accuracy loss, but collapses the tiers when they are trained jointly. Multi-teacher OPD separates them: across 33 benchmarks, low and medium use 58\% and 43\% fewer thinking tokens than high, while high improves on the reference policy by 3.1 points. We trace this difference to the training signal. A sequence-level reward cannot tell tier-specific behavior from shared behavior, whereas OPD supplies a tier-specific target at every visited state, including the decision to stop. Building on this analysis, we show that a single token-level KL anchor on the high tier is sufficient to learn separated tiers within one RL stage, and that natural-language effort instructions make this separation emerge from the start of training, yielding a policy competitive with multi-teacher OPD at matched thinking length. Finally, the separation built by dense targets holds only on the states seen in training: it does not carry over to unseen agentic tasks, where effort acts mainly through the number of interaction turns, whereas the compression induced by sequence-level rewards does.

Title: Verifiable On-Policy Distillation
ArXiv ID: 2611.30325
Date: 2026-11-23
Abstract: Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has become a powerful approach for improving language-model reasoning, but its supervision is inherently sparse: a sequence-level verifier reward is propagated only through sampled trajectories and provides no direct signal for alternative actions. On-policy distillation (OPD) offers a complementary form of supervision by providing dense teacher feedback over the full next-token distribution, but this signal is not grounded in verified task outcomes. Simply co-optimizing the two objectives does not couple these signals: verifier feedback remains sparse and does not inform the dense teacher supervision. We introduce Verifiable On-Policy Distillation (VOPD), which transfers sparse verifier feedback into dense, verifier-grounded supervision. Our key observation is that the implicit-reward view of OPD places teacher supervision and verifier feedback in a common reward space while retaining a reward signal available for every next-token action. Under this view, VOPD projects verifier preference onto the teacher's implicit-reward direction and Rao--Blackwellizes the resulting signal over the full vocabulary, propagating verifier-grounded supervision to actions that were never sampled. The resulting objective can also be interpreted as adaptively extrapolating the distillation target according to teacher--verifier agreement. Across two teacher--student configurations and eight mathematical reasoning benchmarks, VOPD consistently improves both final performance and learning efficiency over vanilla OPD and other on-policy distillation baselines. Our code is available at https://github.com/juntongshi48/VOPD.

Title: Replay, Don't Resample: Off-Policy Consolidation of Long-Horizon RL Experts
ArXiv ID: 2611.24059
Date: 2026-11-18
Abstract: Frontier post-training follows a common recipe: train one expert per domain with reinforcement learning (RL), then consolidate the experts into one model via multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD). Such consolidation is expensive: the student samples in every environment again, an expert scores every sampled token, and the student inherits the drift of the final experts. Yet we argue that the experts' RL runs have already produced what consolidation needs: rollouts with their sampling probabilities and rewards, in the order the experts learned from them. We propose multi-expert replay policy distillation (MRPD), which replays these training logs through the student in the experts' order. At each step, the policy gradient is recomputed on these scored trajectories, and the final experts' distributions are distilled at the logged prefixes. Following the experts' own trajectory reduces the training-inference mismatch and keeps the student within the clip range, so the replay is off-policy in origin but quasi-on-policy throughout consolidation. MRPD samples nothing and calls no environment or reward model: the trainer is the sole process, and consolidating the experts costs only about a seventh of the compute used to train them. On three long-horizon agentic domains (reasoning, search and SQL), MRPD yields the best student model, substantially outperforming online RL baselines, and surpassing MOPD from the same experts at a fraction of MOPD's cost. Our analysis on the same logs shows that the replayed trajectories carry most of the gain, and that both the policy gradient and the distillation term add to it. Most interestingly, the order of the logs matters more than their amount or rewards, suggesting that expert training logs are a curriculum, not a data mixture.

Date: 2026-11-17
Abstract: On-policy distillation (OPD) trains a student language model to match a teacher model based on its own generations. Abstract: Feedback-based on-policy self-distillation has emerged as a promising approach for enabling foundation models, more specifically Large Language Models (LLMs), to learn from their own outputs under external feedback, with a single model serving as both teacher and student. Abstract: Since the advent of knowledge distillation, KL divergence has been the standard loss in distillation. However, in this work, we find that KL divergence may not be necessary for OPD. We show that simply preserving the update direction is sufficient for effective OPD. As long as the update direction is toward the teacher, OPD works. Unlike MOPD, which routes each sample to a single teacher and may cause capability conflicts across domains, C-MOPD lets every sample be supervised by all teachers. Experiments show that C-MOPD consistently outperforms MOPD on both math and code benchmarks. Our code is available at https://github.com/LeapLabTHU/KL-Free-OPD.

Title: What Shared Prefixes Hide: Trajectory Dropout for On-Policy Distillation
ArXiv ID: 2609.33455
Date: 2026-09-27
Abstract: On-policy distillation (OPD) trains a student model on its own trajectories using dense token-level feedback from a stronger teacher model. Since each update is conditioned on the reasoning prefix already generated by the student, the prefix also shapes how effectively teacher feedback is converted into learning. We find that shared prefixes can lead to weak token-level updates, a phenomenon we call Prefix-Induced Supervision Attenuation (PISA). This attenuation arises in two common cases. (i) High student confidence can weaken corrective gradients even when the teacher disagrees. (ii) Tokens that rely on earlier reasoning can receive learning signals as weak as those for simple local continuations. The student first performs a standard full-context rollout to generate a complete trajectory. This intervention strengthens corrections for overconfident predictions and introduces additional supervision at prefix-sensitive positions. Trajectory Dropout consistently improves average performance across teacher--student model pairs of different scales and six mathematical reasoning benchmarks, while also yielding gains on two out-of-domain benchmarks. It can also be flexibly integrated into existing OPD variants with negligible computational overhead, further improving their performance. These results demonstrate that Trajectory Dropout provides a simple mechanism for strengthening token-level supervision across model scales and OPD objectives.
